Step 5 · Have a plan after cancer

Your cancer
survivorship care plan

After cancer treatment, many people leave their last appointment not knowing what comes next. A survivorship care plan gathers in one place what happened, what to watch for and what to do. Ask your oncology team for one, and use this folder to know what to ask.

Diagnosis

What to write down

  • Type of cancer and where it started
  • Date of diagnosis
  • Stage and main characteristics, if your team has shared them
  • Who looks after you: oncologist, GP, other specialists
Ask your team«Could you write me a summary of my diagnosis?»

Treatments

What to write down

  • Surgeries and dates
  • Chemotherapy, immunotherapy or targeted therapy (names and periods)
  • Radiotherapy: area treated and dates
  • Medication you still take
Ask your team«Exactly what treatments did I have and how could they affect me later?»

Late effects

What to write down

  • Possible late effects of your treatment
  • Symptoms to watch (fatigue, pain, tingling, sleep, memory, breathlessness, heart)
  • Warning signs and who to call
Ask your team«Which late effects should I expect, and what are the warning signs?»

Follow-up tests

What to write down

  • Which tests and how often
  • Dates of your next appointments
  • Who orders and who books each test
Ask your team«Which tests do I need, when, and who books them?»

Prevention

What to write down

  • Vaccines to review: flu, COVID-19, pneumococcal and shingles
  • Screening recommended for you
  • Other prevention measures
Ask your team«Which vaccines and screening should I have now?»

Health and wellbeing

What to write down

  • Exercise: 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, plus strength
  • Sleep and nutrition
  • Emotional support: psycho-oncology, groups, family
Ask your team«Are there any restrictions on exercise in my case?»

It is a blank sheet for you to fill in by hand with your team. We do not save or send anything. Survivorship care plans help communication between oncologists, GPs and patients, but they only work if they are used consistently.

Frequently asked questions

Training after cancer

What you need to know about cancer exercise with Paulo Morais, in Lisbon and online.

What is cancer exercise?

It is adapted physical exercise for people living with cancer or after cancer treatment. The plan takes into account physical condition, goals and the healthcare team’s recommendations.

Where can I do cancer exercise with Paulo Morais?

In person in Lisbon or through online training, across Portugal, the European Union and Portuguese-, English- and Spanish-speaking countries.

Is Paulo Morais an oncology clinic?

No. It is an exercise service, not an oncology consultation or cancer treatment. Cancer exercise is one of Paulo Morais’s areas, alongside personal training, group training, online training and osteopathy.

Do I need medical clearance to start?

Taking part may require clearance from, or coordination with, your healthcare team. The exercise assessment considers clinical guidance and relevant limitations before setting training progression.

Can I train during cancer treatment?

Adapted exercise can support different stages, during or after treatment, always in line with the healthcare team’s guidance.

Does the step 0 to 5 guide replace my healthcare team’s plan?

No. The guide is general information to help organise care after cancer and prepare conversations with your healthcare team. It is not a personalised treatment plan.

Which languages are sessions in?

Sessions take place in Portuguese and English. Requests in Spanish, including from Spanish-speaking countries, are confirmed directly.

How do I book an assessment?

You can book an exercise assessment after cancer in the client area, or talk to Paulo by WhatsApp or phone (+351 960 471 537) or by email (pt@pmorais.pt).

Sources cited in this oncology training guide
  • ASCO (American Society of Clinical Oncology): cancer survivorship care as a distinct stage of cancer care, with structured long-term follow-up and survivorship care plans.
  • ASCO: guidance on vaccination in adults with cancer; systematic review of vaccination status (flu, COVID-19, pneumococcal and shingles).
  • Cochrane review in immunosuppressed adults with cancer: flu vaccination is safe; observational data point to lower mortality and better infection-related outcomes.
  • 2023 meta-analysis (28 randomised clinical trials, 1,573 participants): exercise improves cancer-related fatigue and quality of life.
  • Systematic review: fear of cancer recurrence is one of the most frequent and distressing psychosocial problems among survivors.

General information based on the sources above; it is not medical advice and does not replace your oncology team. Paulo Morais adapts exercise to each person; he does not treat cancer or prescribe vaccines or medication.
